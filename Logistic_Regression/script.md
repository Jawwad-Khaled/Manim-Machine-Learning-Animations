## Introduction

Imagine, you are a teacher and you want to know if someone will pass or fail a test based on the number of hours they have studied. This test requires a score of a 70 to pass. You notice that one of your students left a paper on their desk with the test scores and hours studied of the class you took last year. You take a picture of the paper and then plot the data on the paper on a graph. You also know the student who collected the data and are pretty sure that they will fail the test because they never study and you are pretty sure that they are trying to use the data to find out what is the least number of hours they need to study in order to get a 70 because that is what they need to pass.

When plotting the data, you decide that the y-axis should represent the probability of somone passing the test while the x-axis represents the number of hours they studied. When plotting the points, the students who failed get assigned a zero because they already failed so you know that with 100% probability and likewise for the students who passed. Now, you need to decide what could of graph you should plot. Should it be a straight line like for linear regression? Or maybe something entirely different? You also might want to add more predictors like the number of hours a student slept the night before the exam. Then, the graph would be in 3D. Adding even more predictors would make it impossible to visualize the graph. How would you solve this problem?

This is where logistic regression comes in. The thing we are trying to predict isn't some sort of value like a test score. It is an outcome. The outcome of whether a student passed or failed the test. There are also only two possible outcomes as well. The student either passes the test or fails. Since there are only two outcomes, the target, which is the thing we are trying to predict, is a binary variable. The case where you are trying to predict a binary outcome is the perfect case for logistic regression as logistic regression only works on binary variables.

Unlike the linear regression video, we will skip the case with 1 predictor and go straight into the general case where we use matrices. Before we do that though, we will need to figure out how to make sure all of our probabilities are between 0 and 1. We will do this using the sigmoid function. This video assumes that you already watched the linear regression video so things that are covered in more detail in the linear regression video like categorical variables will only be discussed briefly here. However, gradient descent will be covered in much more detail here than the linear regression video as there is no closed form solution to logistic regression.

The example above is not predicting something that resembles a continous value though. It is predicting an outcome. When we are predicting outcomes or classes, we are doing a classification task.

Despite the name, logistic regression is used for binary classification meaning there are only two outcomes or classes and is not used for regression.
